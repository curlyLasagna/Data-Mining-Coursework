# Conditioned Compressed Chain-of-Thought

Save time and money for tokens you didn't ask for with reasoning models!

---

## Problem

The length of text generated by chain of thoughts is significantly greater than the final answer

This is costly in terms of money and time due to the additional inference.

But reasoning steps enhance the performance of LLMs for complex tasks

Implicit-CoT exists as a solution but performs poorly

---

## Background

### Chain of Thought

A prompt engineering technique used in reasoning models where the model generates reasoning steps much like how humans think.

> Calculate the Integral
> 
> I've determined the user's request. It's a definite integral problem: integrating "5x" from 0 to 2. I'm now formulating the integral and preparing to evaluate it using the appropriate rules.
> 
> Calculating the Result
> 
> I've completed the integration and applied the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. The antiderivative of 5x is 5x^2/2. Evaluating this at the limits 2 and 0 yields 10 and 0 respectively. The difference, therefore, is 10, which I'll present clearly, with a breakdown of each step, for the final response.

---

### Implicit-CoT

A reasoning model that internalizes its reasoning steps within its hidden layers instead of generating and showing their steps to the user.

Implicit-CoT is achieved through stepwise internalization.

![](/training_illustration.png){width=400px}

For each stage, a specified number of tokens are removed and the model is tasked to predict those missing tokens and its output.

This continues until all of the tokens are removed.

## Methodology

![](/x1.png){lazy}

---

Given the following dataset: 

$$
D = \{(x_i, \: r_i^{\text{long}},\:y_i)\}_{i=1}^N
$$

- $x_i$: Instructions
- $r_i^{\text{long}}$: Reasoning
- $y_i$: Final answer

Create the following conditioned dataset:

$$
D_c = \{(x_i, \: \tilde{r} \:, y_i\:, c_i)\}_{i=1}^N
$$

Goal is to create a dataset that trains a model the differences and connections between a long and short chain of thought

--- 

### Compressor

A language model is prompted with

You have a question now:

QUESTION:

` Here is Instruction `

THOUGHT PROCESS:

`Here is Original CoT`

ANSWER:

`Here is Final Answer`

Now you need to simplify the THOUGHT PROCESS as short as possible to only include the key information needed to solve the question. And do not add additional information that is not included in the original THOUGHT PROCESS.

--- 

#### Example

$x_i$: Natalia sold clips ... How many clips did Natalia sell altogether in April and May?

$r_i^{\text{long}}$: Natalia sold 48+24 = <<48+24=72>>72 clips altogether in April and May.

Compressor($r_i^{\text{long}}$) $\rightarrow$ She sold 72 clips in April and May.

| $x_i$                                                                               | $r_i^{\text{long}}$                                                    | $r_i^{\text{short}}$                |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Natalia sold clips ... How many clips did Natalia sell altogether in April and May? | Natalia sold 48+24 = <<48+24=72>>72 clips altogether in April and May. | She sold 72 clips in April and May. |

The generated short chain of thought is paired with its longer counterpart 

$$
D = \{(x_i, \: r_i^{\text{long}}, \: r_i^{\text{short}} ,\: y_i)\}_{i=1}^N
$$

---

### Conditioned Training

Train a model to determine the relationship between $r_i^{\text{long}}$ and  $r_i^{\text{short}}$ through a transformed dataset

Append the following prompts:

**Long condition**

> Answer and provide a detailed thought process:

**Short condition**

> Answer and provide as brief a thought process as possible:

Results in the doubling of the dataset

$$
D_c = \{(x_i, \tilde{r_i}, y_i, c_i)\}
$$

---

| **Instruction_ID** | **Condition ($c_i$)** | **Prompt_Prefix**                                          | **Target_CoT ($\tilde r_i$)**                                                                               | **Target_Answer ($y_i$)** |
| ------------------ | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| $X_1$              | long                  | Answer and provide a detailed thought process:             | They used 20 to make lunch. So they had $23 - 20 = 3$. They bought 6 more apples, so they have $3 + 6 = 9$. | 9                         |
| $X_1$              | short                 | Answer and provide as brief a thought process as possible: | Started with 23, used 20, leaving 3. Added 6, total 9.                                                      | 9                         |
| $X_2$              | long                  | Answer and provide a detailed thought process:             | Roger started with 5 balls. 2 cans of 3 tennis balls each is $2 \times 3 = 6$ tennis balls. $5 + 6 = 11$.   | 11                        |
| $X_2$              | short                 | Answer and provide as brief a thought process as possible: | Bought $2 \times 3 = 6$ balls. Total $5 + 6 = 11$.                                                          | 11                        |

---

### Conditioned Inference

The user's prompt has to specify that it wants a brief thought process to trigger the short chain of thought

---

### Evaluation Metrics

- Accuracy
  - Compare the generateed final answer with the truth label from the dataset
- Compression Rate
  - The reduction in length of the generated chain of thought on average across the entire testing sets
  - Larger compression rate $=$ greater reduction in chain of thought, therefore less money spent on inference
  - Value is measured by $\frac{L - \tilde{L}}{L}$
    - $L$: Length of chain of thought
    - $\tilde{L}$: Length of the compressed chain of thought

--- 

## Results

- Outperforms Implicit-CoT
- Math related tasks, which require more reasoning abilities, showed a huge decrease in performance as compression rate increases
- Value of compression rate is affected by the dataset
  - If $r_i^{\text{long}}$ is more detailed, contains more redundant information, then compression rate is higher

### Conditioning Policy

What if we don't implement conditioned training?

Take $D_c$ and remove the prefix prompt tokens and condition so the dataset becomes a mix of:

- $D = \{(x_i, \: r_i^{\text{long}}, \: y_i)\}_{i=1}^N$
- $D = \{(x_i, \: r_i^{\text{short}} ,\: y_i)\}_{i=1}^N$

Models trained without the conditioned dataset had lower compression rates while also being less accurate

### Expanded Chain of Thought

Take the original dataset and expand each reasoning steps with these prompts via GPT-4:

- Think About the Word
- Read the Question Again
- Repeat State
- Self-Verification
- Make Equation

Perform the same methodology for creating a conditioned dataset

Achieved better result compared to the non-expanded version while maintaining a similar compression rate
